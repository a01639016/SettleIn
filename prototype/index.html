EU/EEA citizens

After arrival

Your practical admin checklist.

A prototype checklist based on the SettleIn pilot: the key tasks students commonly need to work through after arriving in Sweden.

0 of 6 admin tasks completedYour progress is saved on this device.
01

Register and get your personnummer

If you move to Sweden and plan to live here for one year or more, you should normally notify Skatteverket and be entered in the Population Register. When first registered, you receive a Swedish personal identity number (personnummer). If you plan to stay for less than one year, different rules apply.

Start after arrivalSkatteverket✓ Official source checked · Sep 2026
Full instructions
  1. Check whether your planned stay is at least one year.
  2. Use Skatteverket's “Moving to Sweden” service and prepare the documents requested for your situation.
  3. Follow Skatteverket's instructions for identity checks or service-centre visits.
  4. If you will stay less than one year, read the short-stay guidance and check whether a coordination number (samordningsnummer) is relevant.
Moving to Sweden · Skatteverket ↗Coordination number ↗
02

Open a Swedish bank account

A Swedish payment account can make rent, salary and everyday payments easier. Banks must verify your identity and may ask where your money comes from and why you need the account. Consumers legally resident in the EEA have a right to a payment account with basic functionality, subject to identity and anti-money-laundering requirements.

Requirements vary by bankBring ID + supporting documents✓ Finansinspektionen guidance · Sep 2026
Full instructions
  1. Compare banks and ask specifically for a payment account with basic functionality.
  2. Bring valid identification and documents that explain your studies, address and expected source/use of funds if requested.
  3. Answer the bank's customer-due-diligence questions completely.
  4. If an account is refused, ask for the written decision and information on how to complain or request review.
Right to an account · FI ↗If a bank refuses you ↗
03

Activate BankID and Swish

BankID is widely used for secure digital identification in Sweden. BankID is issued through participating banks, so eligibility and the exact setup depend on your bank. Swish is a mobile payment service connected to Swedish banking; check your bank's requirements before relying on it.

After bank setupBank requirements apply✓ BankID official information · Sep 2026
Full instructions
  1. Ask your bank whether your account and identification setup qualify for BankID.
  2. Follow your bank's BankID activation process.
  3. Once eligible, check whether your bank supports Swish and connect the service according to its instructions.
  4. Never approve an unexpected BankID request; treat it like a digital signature.
BankID issuing banks ↗BankID official site ↗
04

Understand tax and student work

If you work in Sweden, your tax position depends on factors including residence, length of stay and the type/source of income. For 2026, Skatteverket also has specific guidance for students and young people with low annual employment income.

Before starting workRules depend on your situation✓ Skatteverket · 2026 rules
Full instructions
  1. Check whether you are subject to limited or unlimited Swedish tax liability.
  2. If you take a Swedish job, confirm which tax registration or withholding rules apply before your first salary.
  3. Students with irregular/part-year income should review Skatteverket's student adjustment (jämkning) guidance.
  4. If you are a short-term non-resident worker, check whether SINK rules apply to you.
Tax liability · Skatteverket ↗Tax adjustment for students ↗SINK guidance ↗
05

Set up healthcare

1177 is Sweden's official healthcare information and advice service. In Stockholm, call 1177 when you need medical advice and are unsure where to seek care; call 112 for emergencies. What you pay for care can depend on your country of origin and coverage.

1177 medical advice112 emergencies✓ 1177 / Region Stockholm · Sep 2026
Full instructions
  1. Check what healthcare coverage applies to you as an international student.
  2. Keep your EHIC/GHIC or insurance documentation available if applicable.
  3. Use 1177 to understand where to seek care and to find a healthcare centre (vårdcentral).
  4. For urgent life-threatening situations, call 112.
Healthcare in Stockholm ↗1177 in English ↗
06

Get connected and moving

Set up the mobile service you need and choose the right SL ticket. Adult full-time students can use SL's discounted fare when they meet the study requirements and carry an accepted Swedish student ID showing the SL logo.

After university registrationStudent ID may unlock SL discount✓ SL official guidance · Sep 2026
Full instructions
  1. Activate your university/student identity and check whether your student card displays the SL logo.
  2. Compare SL single tickets and travelcards based on how often you travel.
  3. Keep both your ID and valid student ID available during ticket inspections if using student fare.
  4. Download the SL app for journey planning and mobile tickets.

Cultural integration

Fitting into Stockholm, not just living in it.

Practical context for the social habits and everyday norms that can make the first months easier to understand.

01

Fika is social time

Coffee breaks are often a real opportunity to connect with classmates and colleagues, not simply a coffee run.

02

Quiet can be politeness

Reserved public behaviour can feel unfamiliar at first. Give new relationships time to develop.

03

Student unions matter

University unions, associations and student houses can be useful places to find events, activities and community.

04

Prepare for the dark season

Winter daylight changes significantly. Build routines around activity, social plans and time outdoors.

05

English vs. Swedish

English works in many student settings, while learning basic Swedish can make daily interactions and integration easier.

06

Everyday unwritten rules

Small norms around queues, shared spaces, punctuality and paying separately are useful to learn early.

Housing safety

Spot the red flags before you pay.

International students can be targeted by fake listings and deposit fraud. Use these checks when evaluating housing outside verified providers.

01

Contract before deposit

Be cautious if someone asks you to send money simply to reserve or hold a room before there is a legitimate agreement.

02

Keep the process traceable

Be wary when a supposed provider pushes you away from its normal communication or viewing process, especially for payment.

03

Compare the rent

An unusually cheap central apartment deserves extra verification. Compare similar housing before committing.

04

Protect BankID

Do not use BankID merely because an unknown landlord says it is needed to prove your identity.

05

Verify the provider

Check who owns or manages the listing, use official contact details and confirm the source independently.

Safety guidance adapted from the SettleIn Lovable pilot. The prototype does not guarantee third-party listings.
